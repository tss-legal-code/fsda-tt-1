name: Luxury Hospitality UI
description: Premium editorial visual language for the member-facing luxury travel experience.
---

## Design direction

The member experience should feel like a private luxury travel proposal, not a SaaS dashboard.

Think:

- private travel concierge,
- boutique hospitality,
- editorial travel magazine,
- premium printed itinerary,
- understated sophistication.

The design should communicate exclusivity without looking flashy.

## Visual language

Prefer:

- warm ivory / off-white backgrounds,
- charcoal typography,
- muted stone neutrals,
- restrained champagne / bronze accents,
- generous whitespace,
- elegant typography hierarchy,
- thin subtle borders,
- large editorial imagery,
- subtle texture where appropriate,
- restrained animation.

Avoid:

- neon colors,
- purple SaaS gradients,
- excessive glassmorphism,
- excessive shadows,
- excessive rounded cards,
- excessive badges,
- excessive pills,
- dashboard-style grids,
- overly decorative icons,
- visually noisy backgrounds.

Do not use gold gradients to communicate luxury.

## Typography

Use typography as a major part of the design.

Use:

- a refined serif/display style for major editorial headings,
- a clean sans-serif for body text and controls.

Maintain strong hierarchy between:

- destination,
- member name,
- itinerary dates,
- day headings,
- experiences,
- pricing.

Do not use decorative typography for body copy.

## Composition

Prefer editorial layouts over collections of cards.

For example:

## Imagery

Use imagery selectively.

Images should support the destination and experience rather than decorate every component.

Avoid:

- random stock photos,
- generic travel imagery,
- image-heavy cards everywhere.

If imagery is unavailable, the UI should still look premium without it.

## Pricing

Pricing should be clear but understated.

The total should receive strong visual hierarchy without looking like an e-commerce checkout.

Do not use aggressive sales patterns.

## Buttons

Primary actions should be visually confident but restrained.

Examples:

- Approve Itinerary
- Pay & Lock In

Avoid:

- oversized CTA buttons,
- gradients,
- excessive animation.

## Motion

Use subtle motion for:

- page entrance,
- itinerary transitions,
- approval confirmation,
- payment confirmation.

Animations should reinforce hierarchy and feedback.

Never animate purely for decoration.

## Member experience

The member should understand:

1. where they are going,
2. when they are going,
3. what has been curated for them,
4. what it costs,
5. what action they need to take.

The UI should feel calm and confident.

The member should never feel like they are operating an internal business application.
